# Findings and insights: organisational capacity in community sport clubs

> Years are print-issue years checked against Crossref (all 30 DOIs). Figures reflect
> verification decisions up to paper 5 (e.g. paper 2's *ambition* scales
> are coded "Other", not capacity). They will shift as more papers are checked.

Based on `organised_extraction.xlsx` (30 papers, 481 quantitative variables, 254
limitation and future-research points). Counts that come from the archived raw
extraction (authors, data sources, qualitative themes) are marked *(archive)*.
Figures for limitations and future research come from keyword matching, so treat
them as approximate.

---

## Headline insights

1. **Capacity is mostly studied as a cause, rarely as an effect.** 245 of the 302
   capacity variables are predictors. Capacity is an outcome in only four papers.
   Three of them only compare groups or time points (papers 2, 7 and 26: ANOVA,
   t-tests, Mann-Whitney), and just one models what drives capacity (paper 17,
   Rossi et al., 2023, panel regression). The field asks what capacity does for clubs
   far more than what builds capacity.
2. **The evidence base is heavily German and quantitative.** 17 of 30 papers
   include Germany, and all 12 single-country German papers are quantitative
   surveys. A single research group (Breuer) is a co-author on 13 papers *(archive)*.
3. **Canada supplies almost all of the qualitative work.** 6 of the 7 qualitative
   papers are Canadian, so the qualitative and quantitative evidence come from
   different countries and research traditions.
4. **Cross-sectional designs dominate (25 of 30), yet few papers acknowledge it.**
   Only about 10 papers list cross-sectional design or causality as a limitation,
   while about 14 call for longitudinal work.
5. **Doherty et al.'s (2014) critical elements are mostly used by the people who
   proposed them.** Of the 24 papers published from 2014 onwards, 16 do not use
   them. 4 of the 5 papers that fully use them have Doherty or Cuskelly as a co-author.
6. **External influences have moved from margin to mainstream.** They were
   examined in 3 of 13 papers up to 2018, but in 11 of 17 papers since 2019.
   COVID-19, policy and the wider environment explain much of this rise.
7. **Capacity is measured with simple indicators, not validated scales.** About
   half of the capacity variables are binary dummies (29%) or single
   Likert/rating items (21%). Only three papers (2, 4, 29) develop multi-item scales.

## 1. Publication characteristics

| | |
|---|---|
| Papers | 30, published 2006–2024 |
| Since 2019 | 17 papers (57%), so the field has accelerated recently |
| Journals | 18 different journals. *Journal of Sport Management* and *Sport Management Review* have 5 each, and *Voluntas* has 3 |
| Countries | Germany 17, Canada 9, Belgium 4. 5 papers are multi-country |
| Sport | 23 multi-sport samples. 19 papers do not name the sports. Football (soccer) is the most-named single sport (7 papers) |

- **Concentrated authorship** *(archive)*: Breuer is on 13 papers, Wicker on 8,
  and Doherty, Misener and Feiler on 5 each. The findings therefore lean heavily
  on a few teams' measures and datasets.
- **Shared data sources** *(archive)*: at least 6 papers (8, 15, 17, 18, 26, 27)
  explicitly draw on the German Sport Development Report panel. Papers 21, 22 and
  28 report the same 228 North Rhine-Westphalia clubs, and paper 23 (296 clubs)
  appears to come from the same survey. These papers are not independent samples.
- **Geography and method go together**: Germany means large quantitative surveys,
  Canada means case studies and interviews, and the rest of Europe and Australia
  contribute a handful of studies each.

## 2. Study design

| | Papers |
|---|---|
| Quantitative / Qualitative / Mixed | 22 / 7 / 1 |
| Cross-sectional / Longitudinal / Both | 25 / 4 / 1 |
| Survey: single wave | 14 |
| Survey: clubs + members (multilevel) | 4 |
| Survey: repeated waves (panel) | 3 |
| Case studies | 5 |

- **The field has shifted towards quantitative work.** Up to 2013, 4 of 6 papers
  were qualitative. Since 2019, 14 of 17 have been quantitative (plus one mixed methods).
- **Longitudinal evidence is thin.** Only papers 3, 7, 10, 17 and 26 follow clubs
  over time. Only two (3 and 17) use panel regression; paper 26 compares two
  waves with t-tests.
- **Sample sizes span four orders of magnitude.** Quantitative papers range from
  10 to 30,455 clubs (median about 894). Qualitative papers range from 1 to 51
  (median 6). Large national surveys give breadth, while case studies give the
  only process-level insight into how capacity changes.
- **Club representatives are the main informants.** In most quantitative papers
  one board member reports on the whole club. Only 4 papers (21, 22, 23, 28) also
  survey members, so capacity is rarely seen from below.

## 3. Capacity: what was examined and how

**Dimensions examined (of 30 papers)**

| Human resources | Financial | Infrastructure | Planning | Relationships | External |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 28 | 28 | 26 | 24 | 15 |

- **Human resources is universal; relationships and networks get the least
  attention.** 19 papers cover all five dimensions. Relationship and network
  capacity is the least examined dimension and has the fewest variables (32).
  Only paper 30 studies networks directly.
- **Attention is not the same as measurement depth.** Human resources is examined
  most, but infrastructure has as many predictor variables (76 vs 74), mostly
  facility dummies (own, public or adequate facilities).
- **What gets measured repeatedly** (most common indicators): organisational
  culture, paid staff, club size, facility availability or ownership, strategic
  plans, revenues and financial problems, breaking even, volunteer ratio, and
  cooperation with other organisations.
- **Culture is widely measured but inconsistently placed.** At least 8
  quantitative papers measure organisational culture ("sport for all"
  orientation, tradition, conviviality). Hall et al. (2003) place culture inside
  **Infrastructure and Process Capacity** ("processes and culture"), but papers 6,
  8, 14 and 18 treat it as a separate "cultural capacity" block. Under the codebook
  (CODEBOOK.md §1.1), these variables belong to Infrastructure and Process, which
  would make that dimension even more heavily measured.
- **How capacity was measured (302 capacity variables)**: binary/dummy 29%,
  Likert or rating items 21%, ratios/percentages/shares 18%, categorical bands 6%,
  counts/amounts 6%, composite indices 1%. 18% have no measurement detail recorded.
- **Capacity's role in the analysis** (a variable can have several roles, one per
  analysis): predictor 245, outcome 52 (papers 2, 7, 17, 26), measured only 36.
  See headline 1.

**Critical elements (Doherty et al., 2014)**

| Used | Partly | Not used |
|---|---|---|
| 5 (papers 1, 4, 5, 7, 9) | 3 (14, 15, 16) | 22 |

The partial uses are mostly German papers borrowing single elements (e.g.
"common focus"). Most quantitative work still uses Hall et al.'s broad dimensions
with whatever indicators the survey data offer.

**Qualitative studies** *(archive)*: only 4 of the 8 qualitative and mixed-methods
papers (1, 5, 12, 20) organise their themes by capacity dimension. The rest use
inductive or other framings, which limits direct comparison with the quantitative
evidence.

## 4. Statistical modelling (23 quantitative and mixed papers)

| Model family | Papers |
|---|---|
| Linear regression (OLS) | 8 |
| Multilevel / mixed-effects | 5 |
| Logistic regression | 4 |
| ANOVA / t-test | 4 |
| Correlation / bivariate | 4 |
| Descriptive only | 4 |
| Probit | 2 |
| Factor analysis | 2 |
| Ordered logit, fractional logit, quantile regression, network analysis (QAP) | 1 each |

- **Regression dominates.** Multilevel models appear from 2018 onwards, mainly
  for member-in-club data (papers 21–23) and cross-country data (paper 19).
- **No study uses structural equation modelling or mediation analysis**, even
  though capacity is theorised as latent dimensions that work through one another.
  This is a clear methodological gap.
- **Few designs support causal claims.** There is one quasi-experimental design
  (paper 7), two panel analyses (3 and 17) and no interventions.

The limitations and the calls for future research mirror each other. Authors
repeatedly ask for multi-perspective, longitudinal and cross-national work with
better measures, which is exactly what the evidence base lacks.

## Gaps your review can highlight

1. **Capacity as an outcome:** what builds or erodes club capacity over time?
2. **Longitudinal and causal designs:** panel models, natural experiments,
   evaluations of capacity-building interventions.
3. **Validated, multi-item capacity measures** (building on papers 2, 4 and 29),
   and a consistent way of measuring the critical elements.
4. **Relationship and network capacity**, the least studied dimension.
5. **Beyond Germany and Canada**, and beyond a single board respondent per club.
6. **Linking the qualitative and quantitative evidence**, which currently come
   from different countries and frameworks.
7. **Organisational culture**, which is widely measured, but often outside the
   Infrastructure and Process dimension where Hall et al. (2003) place it.
